Iroko Framework: Nkisi Module (Spiritual Entities)

Vocabulary for documenting spiritual entities across Afro-Atlantic traditions. Covers orisa, lwa, mpungo/inquice, vodun, egun, ajogun, nature spirits, Espiritismo guides and commissions, and Ori. Supports path/camino modeling, shrine documentation, and syncretic identity tracking.

Path

https://iroko-framework.github.io/iroko-framework/vocab/iroko-nkisi#Path
CLASS

A distinct manifestation, aspect, or camino of a spiritual entity. Paths share the parent entity's fundamental nature but have distinct domains, temperaments, colors, numbers, offerings, and sometimes distinct syncretic identities. Example: Oya Yansa (fierce warrior aspect) vs. Oya Igbale (governess of the cemetery). Paths are not separate entities but distinct faces of the same power.

Label:
"Path"@en

Concept Schemes

Commission Type Classification

https://iroko-framework.github.io/iroko-framework/vocab/iroko-nkisi#CommissionTypeScheme

Named commissions (comisiones) in Espiritismo. Each commission represents a group of spirits sharing a common historical, cultural, or archetypal identity. Documented on espiritu-commission entities via nkisi:commissionType.

Africano Commission of African spirits, often depicted as enslaved Africans or African elders. Work with strength, protection, and grounding. Among the most powerful commissions.
Arabe Commission of Arab or Moorish spirits. Work with commerce, prosperity, and esoteric knowledge. Reflect historical Moorish presence in Iberian culture transmitted to the Caribbean. Also: Arab, Moro
Celestial Commission Commission of highly evolved or elevated spirits associated with light, purity, and spiritual advancement. May include spirits identified as angels or exalted ancestors. Also: Comision Celestial
Congo Commission of Kongo-derived spirits in Espiritismo. Work with earthy, transformative, and protective matters. Bridge between Espiritismo and Palo traditions in syncretic practice.
Gitano Commission of Romani spirits. Work with divination, luck, commerce, and love. Often approached for card reading and matters of fortune. Also: Gypsy
Indio Commission of Indigenous American spirits. Work with nature, healing, and the land. Common in Cuban, Puerto Rican, and Dominican Espiritismo reflecting indigenous Taino presence. Also: Native American
Intranquil Spirit Spirits who died in conflict, trauma, or without resolution. Worked cautiously; associated with unrest and unfinished business. Distinct from the other commissions in that they are engaged for specific workings rather than as ongoing guides. Also: Espiritu Intranquilo
Madama Commission of maternal African or Afro-Caribbean women spirits, often depicted as market women or healers. Work with protection, home, healing, and practical matters.
Marinero Commission of sailor or seafaring spirits. Work with travel, navigation, and matters of the sea. Reflect Caribbean maritime culture. Also: Sailor
Medical Commission Commission of spirits who were doctors, healers, or medical practitioners in life. Work with healing and health matters. Sometimes called upon in misa for diagnosis. Also: Comision Medica

Entity Type Classification

https://iroko-framework.github.io/iroko-framework/vocab/iroko-nkisi#EntityTypeScheme

Classification of spiritual entity types across Afro-Atlantic traditions. Organized by ontological category: divine powers, personal essence, ancestral spirits, nature spirits, and adversarial forces.

Ajogun Adversarial cosmic forces in Yoruba cosmology. The principal Ajogun are Iku (Death), Arun (Disease), Ofo (Loss), Egba (Paralysis), Oran (Legal Trouble), Epe (Curse), Ewon (Imprisonment), and Ese (all other afflictions). Powerful forces that are propitiated or deflected rather than worshipped. Not deities; not ancestors; ontologically distinct from orisa. Also: Adversarial Force
Egun The ancestral dead across Afro-Atlantic traditions. Not tradition-specific: encompasses egungún (Yoruba), the dead honored at boveda (Espiritismo), ancestral courts in Vodou, and the honored dead in all traditions where ancestors are engaged. The dead precede and supersede the orisa and lwa in most traditions. Also: The Dead, Ancestors, Eggun
Espiritu Guia A personal spirit guide in the Espiritismo tradition. Part of an individual's cuadro espiritual (spiritual frame). Guides may be ancestral relatives, historical figures, or evolved spirits assigned to assist a specific person. Identified through mediumship and misa espiritual. Also: Spirit Guide, Guide
Espiritu de Commission A spirit belonging to a named commission (comision) in Espiritismo. Commissions are groups of spirits sharing a common historical, cultural, or archetypal identity. Commission type is documented via nkisi:commissionType property and the CommissionTypeScheme. Also: Commission Spirit
Lwa (Ghede) Spirits of the Ghede nanchon in Haitian Vodou. Govern death, sexuality, resurrection, and the boundary between living and dead. Liminal in temperament. Composite New World origins distinct from both Rada and Petwo. Also: Loa Gede, Gede
Lwa (Petwo) Spirits of the Petwo nanchon in Haitian Vodou. Hot, fierce, and transformative in temperament. Kongo, New World maroon, and creole origins. Associated with fire and gunpowder. Also: Loa Petro
Lwa (Rada) Spirits of the Rada nanchon in Haitian Vodou. Generally cool, sweet, and beneficent in temperament. Primary Fon/Dahomey origins. Received with white and light colors. Also: Loa Rada
Mpungo/Inquice Divine forces in Bantu-derived traditions: Mpungo in Palo Monte and Mayombe, Inquice in Candomble Angola. These are the same cosmic powers known by different names in the Kongo religious continuum. Example: Zarabanda (Palo) corresponds to Ogum (Candomble Angola). Also: Mpungu, Inquice
Nfumbe The human dead bound to a nganga in Palo Monte and Mayombe. Ontologically distinct from free ancestors (egun): the nfumbe enters a specific contractual and coercive relationship with the Palero and the nganga vessel. The nfumbe works within the nganga and is not free.
Ori The individual divine head and personal destiny in Yoruba and Yoruba-derived traditions. Not an external spirit but the innermost self and personal divine principle. Ori has a shrine (ile ori), receives rogacion and propitiation, and is considered the most important force in an individual's life. Its guidance supersedes that of the orisa.
Orisa Divine forces in Yoruba and Yoruba-derived traditions (Lucumi, Candomble Ketu, Trinidad Orisha, Oyotunji). Orisa are cosmic powers governing natural and human domains, each with specific attributes, offerings, colors, and numbers. Also: Orisha, Orixa
Spirit of Element Spirits governing elemental forces — water, wind, fire, earth, lightning — not otherwise classified as orisa, lwa, or other named divine powers. Includes elemental presences recognized in folk traditions and syncretic contexts. Also: Elemental Spirit
Spirit of Place Spirits tied to specific locations, landscapes, or natural features: forests, rivers, crossroads, mountains, specific trees or stones. Not the dead, not divine powers, not malevolent by nature. Engaged through offering and acknowledgment. Found across multiple traditions including Hoodoo and syncretic folk practices. Also: Faires, Woodland Spirit, Spirit of the Land
Vodun Divine forces in Fon/Ewe-derived traditions including Arara in Cuba. Related to but distinct from Haitian lwa; represents the continental Dahomean tradition. Also: Vodu

Offering Type Classification

General categories of offerings given to spiritual entities. Specific quantities and preparation are access-restricted; these general categories are public.

Animal Animal sacrifice or offering. Category is public; specific animals, method, and context are initiated-only.
Candle/Light Candles or other light sources offered or burned for an entity.
Cloth/Fabric Fabric, beads, or clothing offered or draped for an entity in the entity's sacred colors.
Drink Liquids offered to an entity: water, rum, wine, honey, etc.
Food Prepared or natural foods offered to an entity. Specific foods are tradition and entity specific.
Labor/Action Dedicated labor, service, or action performed as offering. Example: building a shrine, completing a vow, acts of charity associated with an entity's domain.
Money/Coin Coins or currency offered to an entity, particularly common with Elegba, Oshun, and market-oriented spirits.
Music/Song Songs, drumming, or music performed in honor of an entity. General category is public; specific songs and rhythms are initiated-only.
Plant/Herb Plants, flowers, or herbal preparations offered to an entity. Links to ewe:Plant instances.
Sacred Object Ritual objects, implements, or symbolic items offered or dedicated to an entity.
Water Fresh water, ocean water, river water, or blessed water offered to an entity.

Polarity and Gender Modality

https://iroko-framework.github.io/iroko-framework/vocab/iroko-nkisi#PolarityModalityScheme

Controlled terms for describing metaphysical polarity or gendered alignment in ritual address, cosmology, and manifestation. This scheme does not describe biological sex. Multiple concepts may apply to a single entity via different paths (iroko:RelationshipAssertion with assertionScope for camino-level distinctions).

Androgynous Masculine and feminine qualities are simultaneously present and inseparable. Distinct from aspect-dependent, where polarity shifts between manifestations.
Apolar Not described in gendered or polarity terms, or explicitly transcending the masculine/feminine binary. Not the same as unknown. Also: Beyond polarity
Aspect-Dependent Polarity varies by named camino, avatar, path, or manifestation. Individual paths should carry their own polarity assertion via iroko:RelationshipAssertion with assertionScope. Also: Camino-dependent
Feminine Consistent feminine polarity in ritual address, manifestation, and alignment across known caminos.
Masculine Consistent masculine polarity in ritual address, manifestation, and alignment across known caminos.
Temporal Polarity shifts according to time of year, liturgical calendar, season, or ritual period. The shift is cyclical and predictable, not camino-specific. Also: Seasonal polarity shift

Temperament Classification

https://iroko-framework.github.io/iroko-framework/vocab/iroko-nkisi#TemperamentScheme

Temperament or spiritual disposition of an entity or path. Reflects the nature and quality of the entity's energy and how it is approached in ritual.

Cool/Sweet Entities of cool, gentle, beneficent energy. Approached with white and light colors, cool water, and gentle offerings. Example: Obatala, Yemaya (certain paths), Rada lwa. Also: Frio, Rada
Hot/Fierce Entities of hot, intense, warrior energy. Approached with fire, iron, rum, and strong offerings. Example: Ogun, Shango, Petwo lwa. Also: Caliente, Petwo
Liminal Entities who exist at thresholds — between life and death, sacred and profane, this world and the next. Example: Ghede lwa, Eshu/Elegba as gatekeeper, Oya Igbale.
Transformative Entities whose primary nature is change, transition, and transformation. Neither simply cool nor hot; work with both destruction and creation. Example: Oya, Elegba.
