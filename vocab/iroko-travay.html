Iroko Framework: Travay Module (Ritual Processes)

Vocabulary for documenting ritual processes in Afro-Atlantic traditions. Covers ceremonies, initiatory rites, and ceremonial sequences. Distinguishes ongoing ceremonial practice from threshold initiation events. Access control is at the property level — the existence and general nature of ceremonies is public; operational sequences and details are restricted.

Initiatory Rite

https://iroko-framework.github.io/iroko-framework/vocab/iroko-travay#InitiatoryRite
CLASS

A threshold ceremony that permanently changes the initiant's spiritual status. Examples: kariocha (making Ocha), mano de Orula, abo faca, pinaldo, rayamiento (Palo), kanzo (Vodou), feitura (Candomble). Initiatory rites are non-repeatable transformations. Operational details are initiated-elder access.

Label:
"Initiatory Rite"@en
Subclass Of:
RitualProcess

Concept Schemes

Authority Level Classification

https://iroko-framework.github.io/iroko-framework/vocab/iroko-travay#AuthorityLevelScheme

Level of initiation or authority required to lead or perform a ritual process.

Any Practitioner Can be performed by any person regardless of initiation status. Example: basic ancestor veneration at boveda, spiritual baths using common herbs.
Babalawo / Ifa Priest Requires Ifa initiation specifically. Certain ceremonies and all Ifa-based divination require Babalawo authority.
Elder Designation Requires recognition as an elder or authority holder within the tradition, beyond basic initiation. Community recognition is required in addition to initiation level.
Initiated Practitioner Requires initiation but not a specific level or title. General initiation into the tradition is sufficient.
Olorisha / Equivalent Requires full head initiation: kariocha in Lucumi, kanzo in Vodou, feitura in Candomble, or equivalent in other traditions. Also: Head-initiated
Tradition-Specific Authority Authority requirements are tradition-specific and cannot be generalized. Document via travay:operationalNotes with tradition context.

Initiation Level Classification

https://iroko-framework.github.io/iroko-framework/vocab/iroko-travay#InitiationLevelScheme

Levels of initiation conferred by initiatory rites. General structure is cross-traditional; specific levels are tradition-dependent.

Head Initiation Full head initiation permanently establishing the initiant as a priest or priestess. Specific name varies by tradition. Also: Kariocha, Kanzo, Feitura, Rayamiento
Ifa Initiation Initiation into the Ifa priesthood. Mano de Orula (hand of Orula) is a preliminary in Lucumi; full Babalawo initiation is the complete level. Also: Babalawo initiation, Mano de Orula
Necklaces/Elekes Reception of consecrated beaded necklaces in Lucumi tradition. First formal step. Confers relationship with specific orisa represented. Also: Collares
Reception Level General category for receiving sacred objects or marks without full head initiation. The entry level of formal initiation in most traditions.
Tradition-Specific Level Initiation levels that do not map to general categories. Document with tradition context via iroko:tradition and travay:operationalNotes.
Warriors Reception of Eleggua, Ogun, Ochosi, and Osun in Lucumi tradition. Protects the initiant and opens roads. Prerequisite for kariocha. Also: Los Guerreros

Participant Role Classification

https://iroko-framework.github.io/iroko-framework/vocab/iroko-travay#ParticipantRoleScheme

Roles that participants may hold within a ritual process.

Co-Officiant A supporting officiant with authority to perform portions of the ceremony.
Community Member General attendee, potentially uninitiated, present for community portions of the ceremony.
Godparent/Sponsor The initiated sponsor responsible for the initiant's spiritual formation and ceremonial participation. Also: Padrino, Madrina, Parrain, Marraine
Helper/Aide Initiated helper assisting with logistical or supporting ceremonial tasks.
Initiant The person receiving initiation or the primary recipient of the ceremony. Also: Iyawo, Initiate
Lead Officiant The principal authority leading the ceremony. Title varies by tradition. Also: Oriaté, Houngan, Mambo, Tata
Medium Practitioner who may receive spiritual possession during the ceremony. In Espiritismo context, medium for spirit communication. Also: Horse, Chwal
Musician/Drummer Drummer, singer, or musician providing ceremonial music. May have specific initiation requirements depending on tradition and ceremony type. Also: Akpwon, Tambolero
Witness An initiated practitioner present to witness and validate the ceremony.

Process Type Classification

https://iroko-framework.github.io/iroko-framework/vocab/iroko-travay#ProcessTypeScheme

Classification of ritual process types across Afro-Atlantic traditions.

Ancestor Veneration Ceremonies specifically directed to the ancestral dead. Includes misa espiritual (Espiritismo), egungún masquerade, boveda work, and ituto (funeral rites for initiates). Also: Misa Espiritual, Egun ceremony
Cleansing/Purification Ritual cleansing of a person, object, or space. Removes negative energies, spiritual impurities, or adversarial influences. Includes despojos, limpiezas, spiritual baths, and space cleansings. Also: Despojo, Limpieza
Community Working Collective ceremonial work on behalf of a house, lineage, or community rather than an individual. Includes cabildo ceremonies, community ebbos, and house-level divination.
Divination Ritual consultation for guidance. Includes Ifa consultation, dilogun reading, chamalongos in Palo, and card reading in Espiritismo context. Public category; specific methods and interpretations are access-restricted.
Elevation/Levantamiento Ceremony to elevate the spirit of a deceased person or clear unresolved spiritual burdens. Common in Espiritismo and Lucumi death rites.
Feast/Celebration Celebration ceremonies: anniversary of initiation, feast days of entities, communal celebrations. Also: Cumpleanos de Santo
Funerary Death and funeral ceremonies specific to initiated practitioners. Includes ituto (Lucumi), axexe (Candomble), and rites handling spiritual obligations of deceased initiates. Also: Ituto, Axexe
Head Feeding/Rogation Ritual feeding and cooling of the head (Ori) with white foods and prayers. Strengthens personal destiny and calms spiritual disturbances. One of the most fundamental ceremonies across Yoruba-derived traditions. Also: Rogacion de Cabeza
Healing Ceremonial processes directed toward physical, spiritual, or psychological healing. May combine plant use, prayer, divination, and direct spiritual intervention.
Initiation - Full Full head initiation permanently transforming the initiant's spiritual status. Examples: kariocha (Lucumi), kanzo (Vodou), rayamiento (Palo), feitura (Candomble). The most restricted process type. Also: Head Initiation
Initiation - Reception Reception of sacred objects conferring a level of initiation without full head initiation. Examples: receiving Warriors, receiving Olokun, receiving necklaces. Creates formal relationship with specific entities. Also: Receiving
Possession Ceremony Ceremony in which spirits may manifest through initiated practitioners. Includes tambor, bembe, wemilere (Lucumi), Vodou fet, Candomble xire. Drumming, singing, and dance are central. Also: Bembe, Tambor, Wemilere, Fet
Protection Ceremonial processes establishing or strengthening spiritual protection for a person, household, or space.
Sacrifice/Major Offering Ceremonies involving animal sacrifice or major offerings to spiritual entities. General category is public; specific animals, quantities, and procedures are initiated-only or initiated-elder. Also: Ebbo, Matanza

Timing Requirement Classification

https://iroko-framework.github.io/iroko-framework/vocab/iroko-travay#TimingRequirementScheme

Whether a ritual process has specific timing requirements.

Annual Cycle Performed on an annual cycle, such as anniversary of initiation.
Any Time No specific timing requirement; may be performed as needed.
Calendar/Feast Day Associated with a specific calendar date or feast day of the presiding entity.
Divination-Prescribed Timing determined through divination consultation. The appropriate time is revealed rather than predetermined.
Emergency/As Needed Performed in response to urgent spiritual or physical crisis without time constraint.
Life Event Triggered by a specific life event: birth, death, marriage, illness, major transition.
Lunar Phase Must be performed during a specific phase of the moon.
