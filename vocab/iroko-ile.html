Cabildo

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/ile#Cabildo
CLASS

A Cuban colonial-era mutual aid and religious society organized by African ethnic identity. Cabildos were the institutional vessels for Lucumí, Arará, Kongo, and other traditions in Cuba. Distinct from contemporary houses in legal form and historical structure. May have successor houses linked via ile:successorHouse.

Label:
"Cabildo"@en
Subclass Of:
House

House

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/ile#House
CLASS

A religious house, temple, or community organized around a lineage and founding elder. Encompasses ilé (Lucumí/Candomblé), terreiro (Candomblé/Umbanda), hounfò (Vodou), ile orisha, and analogous institutions. Subclass of schema:Organization for interoperability with external directories.

Label:
"House"@en
Subclass Of:
SacredEntity
Organization

Initiation Event

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/ile#InitiationEvent
CLASS

A specific ceremony creating a formal genealogical link between initiator and initiate. The primary mechanism by which lineage is transmitted and authority is conferred. Access to records is typically initiated-only; existence of the event may be public.

Lineage

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/ile#Lineage
CLASS

A rama, branch, or transmission line descending from a founding house or practitioner. Represents what makes a specific lineage distinct from others within the same tradition: liturgical variations, oriki, priestly protocols, or founding relationships.

Religious Authority

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/ile#Authority
CLASS

A religious title or position held by a practitioner, authorizing specific ritual functions within a tradition. Linked to the person (foaf:Person), the house, and the authority figure who conferred it. Distinct from iroko:ContestedAuthority in core, which models disputed titles at the category level — ile:Authority models specific individual instances.

Label:
"Religious Authority"@en
Subclass Of:
SacredEntity

Schism Event

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/ile#SchismEvent
CLASS

A documented lineage split creating a new house from an existing one. Records the parent house, child house, date, cause, and parties involved. Often sensitive; access level is typically community-restricted or initiated-only.

Concept Schemes

Governance Models

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/ile#GovernanceModelScheme

Age-Grade Authority structured by seniority within initiation grade rather than individual title.
Democratic / Consensus Decisions made by member consensus or elected leadership.
Elder Council Collective of recognized elders sharing governance authority.
Gender-Divided Authority Male and female authorities govern separate domains within the same institution.
Hereditary Leadership passes through biological or ritual family succession.
Lineage Federation Federated structure of affiliated houses under loose shared authority.
Other
Single Elder Authority One recognized head (Iyalosha, Babalosha, Mambo, etc.) with primary authority.

House Types

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/ile#HouseTypeScheme

Abakuá Potencia An Abakuá lodge (potencia). Abakuá is a Cuban secret society of Calabari/Cross River origin.
Cabildo Cuban colonial mutual aid and religious society organized by African ethnic identity. Use ile:Cabildo subclass for records of this type.
Egbé Association or society organized around a specific orisha or shared ritual purpose.
Espiritismo Centre Centre for Kardecist or Afro-influenced Espiritismo practice.
Hounfò Haitian Vodou temple and its associated community.
Ilé Ifá House organized primarily around Ifá divination practice and Babaláwo lineage.
Ilé Orisha Lucumí/Candomblé Ketu house organized around Orisha worship.
Other
Palo House House organized around Palo Monte/Mayombe practice and nganga custody.
Sosyete Haitian Vodou society, often with named lineage and territorial identity.
Study / Learning Group An informal group organized for learning, not yet constituted as a full house.
Terreiro Brazilian ritual space for Candomblé, Umbanda, or Quimbanda practice.

Initiation Types

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/ile#InitiationTypeScheme

Abakuá Initiation Initiation into an Abakuá potencia.
Couche The lying-in period component of Vodou kanzo initiation.
Egbé Initiation Initiation into an Egbé society or association.
Ifá Initiation Full initiation into the Ifá corpus, conferring the title of Babaláwo. Also: Itefa
Kanzo Haitian Vodou initiation ceremony. Levels include Sur Pwen, Hounsi Kanzo, Si Pwen, and Asogwe.
Kariosha Full head initiation in Lucumí/Candomblé Ketu. An orisha is seated on the initiate's head. Also: Asiento, Making Ocha
Mano de Orula Reception of Orula's hand. First level of Ifá initiation, open to non-Babaláwo. Also: Awofakan / Ikofa
Necklaces Reception of the five sacred beaded necklaces. Often the first formal initiation in Lucumí. Also: Collares, Elekes
Olofin Initiation Reception of the Olofin mysteries. Among the highest grades in Lucumí practice.
Other
Rayamiento Palo Monte/Mayombe initiation ceremony marking entry into a munanso. Also: Palo Initiation
Rayisman Haitian Vodou ceremony of recognition and elevation within a sosyete.
Warriors Reception of the warrior orishas: Elegua, Ogun, Ochosi, and Osun. Also: Los Guerreros

Knowledge Transmission Types

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/ile#TransmissionTypeScheme

Combined Transmission involves both embodied/oral and written components.
Divination-Prescribed The specific form of transmission was determined by divination rather than fixed protocol.
Embodied Knowledge transmitted through physical ceremony, practice, and direct experiential transmission. Cannot be fully conveyed through text.
Oral Transmitted through speech, song, proverb, and oral recitation.
Written Transmitted through notebooks, libreta, manuscripts, or printed texts.

Religious Authority Titles

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/ile#AuthorityTitleScheme

Abakuá Member Initiated member of an Abakuá potencia.
Alagba Senior elder in a Yoruba-derived tradition, often with lineage-wide ceremonial authority.
Araba Chief Babaláwo in a Yoruba community or lineage. Highest recognized Ifá authority.
Babalosha Senior male Orisha priest with the authority to initiate others. Also: Babalorishaisha, Padrino
Babaláwo Initiated Ifá priest. Authorized to perform Ifá divination and related ceremonies.
Ekpe Member Initiated member of the Ekpe leopard society (Cross River / Calabari tradition).
Houngan Male Haitian Vodou priest.
Iyalosha Senior female Orisha priest with the authority to initiate others. Also: Iyalorisha, Madrina
Iyanifa Female Ifá priest. Recognition is contested across lineages; use iroko:contestedStatus when applicable.
Mambo Female Haitian Vodou priest.
Mãe de Santo Female head of a Brazilian terreiro.
Olofista Priest initiated into the Olofin mysteries. Among the highest initiatory grades in Lucumí.
Olorisha Initiated Orisha priest of any gender. General term covering Iyalosha and Babalosha.
Olu-Iroko Consecration to the Iroko tree spirit. Rare; received through specific lineage authorization.
Omo Orisha Initiated child of an orisha. General term for those who have completed kariosha/asiento.
Osugbo Member Member of the Osugbo/Ogboni earth society, holding judicial and spiritual authority. Also: Ogboni
Other
Pai de Santo Male head of a Brazilian terreiro.
Tata Nkisi Male initiated priest in Palo Monte with authority over an nganga.
Yaya Nkisi Female initiated priest in Palo Monte with authority over an nganga.
