Iroko Marca: Divination Systems and Sacred Sign Corpora

Vocabulary for documenting divination systems, sacred signs, reading records, and verse corpora across Afro-Atlantic traditions. Covers Ifá, Dilogún, Fá, Obi, Chamalongo, and analogous systems. Access control is applied at the property level: sign names and system typologies are public; verse texts, reading details, and prescribed works are restricted.

Divination Corpus

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/marca#DivinationCorpus
CLASS

The complete body of verses, prescriptions, proverbs, and interpretive commentary associated with a divination system as transmitted through a specific lineage. Distinct from the system itself (which is the abstract framework) and from individual verses (which are its units). A corpus is lineage-specific: Ifá lineages in Havana may hold different verse inventories than those in Lagos or Trinidad, each representing a distinct corpus.

Divination Reading

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/marca#DivinationReading
CLASS

A specific divination event performed for a recipient by an authorized diviner. Records the sign(s) that appeared, the outcome polarity (iré/osogbo), prescribed works, and the entities indicated. Reading records are among the most sensitive data in the framework: recipient identity is no-access; the date and officiant are community-restricted; the prescribed work and entity are initiated-only.

Divination Sign

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/marca#DivinationSign
CLASS

A discrete sign, figure, or configuration within a divination system. In Ifá: one of 256 odù (each identified by primary + secondary throw). In Dilogún: one of 16 principal signs (odù Dilogún, omieros, and their paths). In Fá: du figures. In Chamalongo: one of 5 configurations. Each sign has an associated corpus of verses, prescriptions, and spirit-entity relationships. The sign's name and number are public; verses and prescribed works are restricted.

Divination System

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/marca#DivinationSystem
CLASS

A tradition-specific divination system consisting of a defined corpus of signs, casting methods, and interpretive frameworks. Examples: Ifá (256 odù, palm nut or chain casting), Dilogún (16 principal signs, cowrie casting), Fá (Fon parallel to Ifá), Obi (4-part binary coconut or kola reading), Chamalongo (Palo Kongo coconut/bone divination). A DivinationSystem belongs to one or more traditions and may have regional variants.

Divination Verse

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/marca#DivinationVerse
CLASS

A single verse, story, or pataki within a sign's corpus. In Ifá: an ese Ifá. In Dilogún: a rezo or pataki. In Fá: a hwenoho. Verses carry narrative, ethical, prescriptive, and esoteric content. They are the primary means by which divination knowledge is transmitted and applied. Verse text is initiated-elder access; the existence of a verse corpus for a sign is public.

Concept Schemes

Casting Media

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/marca#CastingMediumScheme

Chamalongo (coconut shells) Four coconut shell halves prepared and consecrated for Chamalongo divination in Palo traditions.
Chamalongo (prepared bones/discs) Prepared bones or discs used as an alternative casting medium for Chamalongo. The specific preparation is lineage-specific and restricted.
Ikin (palm nuts) Sacred palm nuts (ikin Ifá, obi Ifá) used by Babalawos for Ifá divination. Considered the highest-authority casting medium in Lucumí/Yorùbá tradition.
Obi Abata (coconut) Coconut divided into four pieces for Obi divination.
Obi Kola (kola nut) Kola nut divided into lobes for Obi divination. More traditional form in continental Yorùbá practice.
Okuele / Ekuele (divining chain) Eight-piece divining chain of alternating concave and convex seed halves used by Babalawos as a faster alternative to palm nut casting in Ifá.
Other Casting medium not covered by existing concepts.
Owó Dilogún (cowrie shells) Sixteen cowrie shells used in Dilogún divination by authorized Olorisha.

Divination Outcome Types

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/marca#OutcomeTypeScheme

Iré (positive) A sign comes with positive polarity. The indicated blessings are accessible. Prescribed works (ebó) confirm and open the path.
Iré Aiku (long life) Iré of long life. The sign comes with the blessing of longevity.
Iré Aré (victory) Iré of victory or achievement. The sign comes with the blessing of success in endeavor.
Iré Omo (children) Iré of children or generativity. The sign comes with the blessing of progeny or creative fruitfulness.
Iré Owó (wealth) Iré of prosperity. The sign comes with the blessing of material abundance.
Osogbo (negative) A sign comes with negative polarity. The indicated difficulties or blockages are present. Prescribed works (ebó) are required to avert or mitigate.
Osogbo Arun (illness) Osogbo of illness or physical affliction.
Osogbo Ikú (death) Osogbo of death or severe loss. The most serious category; requires immediate and specific ebó.
Osogbo Ojú (enmity) Osogbo of enemies or opposition. The negative force indicated involves interpersonal conflict or hostility.

Divination System Types

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/marca#DivinationSystemScheme

Chamalongo Kongo-derived divination using four coconut shell halves or prepared bones/discs. Generates 5 configurations. Used in Palo Monte and related Bantu-Atlantic traditions. Practiced by authorized Paleros (Tatas and Yayas).
Derlé Card-based or object-based divination associated with Espiritismo and Haitian Vodou mesa blanca practice. Distinct from African-derived binary casting systems.
Dilogún Cowrie shell divination using 16 shells (owó dilogún) to generate 16 principal signs. Practiced by authorized Olorisha. Used in Lucumí/Ocha, Candomblé, and related traditions. Shares sign names with Ifá odù but distinct system, corpus, and authority structure.
Fá The Fon and Ewe divination system cognate to Ifá, using palm nuts or a divining chain to generate du figures. Practiced by Bokonon (male diviners). The du corpus is distinct from but related to Yorùbá odù. Primary in Benin, Togo, and Haitian Vodou communities with Rada/Fon lineages.
Ifá The Yorùbá-derived divination system using ikin (palm nuts) or okuele (divining chain) to generate 256 binary odù configurations. Practiced by authorized Babalawos. Versions found in Lucumí (Cuba), Candomblé de Ifá (Brazil), Trinidadian Orisha, and continental Yorùbá communities.
Itá The extended Ifá reading performed for an individual at initiation (kariosha, itá de kariosha) or as a life-path consultation. Typically multi-sign, conducted by a Babalawo, and interpreted by Olorisha elders. The itá record is among the most sensitive personal documents in the tradition.
Obi Four-piece binary divination using coconut (obi abata) or kola nut (obi kola). Generates 5 configurations from the binary state of four pieces. Used across Lucumí, Candomblé, and Yorùbá traditions for confirmation and yes/no queries. Less elaborate than Ifá or Dilogún but widely practiced.
Other Divination system not covered by existing concepts. Use skos:scopeNote to describe.
Vititi Nfinda Mirror or reflective-surface divination used in Palo Monte for spiritual vision and consultation. Complementary to but distinct from Chamalongo; requires specific nganga authorization.

Prescribed Work Types

https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/marca#EboTypeScheme

Abstention / Prohibition A behavioral abstention or avoidance prescribed by the reading: specific foods, colors, locations, or activities to avoid for a defined period.
Adimu A food or vegetable offering (without blood) to an Orisha. Accessible outside of full ebó; considered the first step in propitiation.
Animal sacrifice Ritual sacrifice of an animal specified by the reading. Species, preparation, and allocation are operational details held at initiated-only access.
Ebó A prescribed offering to the Orisha or Ifá indicated by the reading. Includes specific materials, animals, foods, or objects. Operational details are initiated-only.
Full ceremony A prescribed ceremony (initiation, reception of Orisha, specific feast) indicated by the reading as necessary.
Itutu / Etutu A cooling or pacifying ceremony, often performed after death, severe osogbo, or to address Egun. Requires specific authority and materials.
Rogación de cabeza A cleansing and feeding of the head (ori/eleda) using specific cool and white materials. One of the most commonly prescribed works across Lucumí and related traditions.
Spiritual cleansing A full-body or environmental cleansing (despojo, limpieza) using herbs, waters, or other materials prescribed by the reading.
