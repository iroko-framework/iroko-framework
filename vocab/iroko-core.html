Core Vocabulary

Cross-module governance infrastructure

iroko: https://www.irokosociety.org/iroko-framework/core# v1.0.0 Download TTL ↓ GitHub ↗

Cross-module infrastructure for governance and semantic integration. Provides access control, provenance tracking, contested knowledge modeling, relationship assertion reification, sacred media metadata, and semantic linking capabilities. All other Iroko modules depend on this vocabulary.

Classes 7 classes
ContestedAuthority
Contested Authority

An authority (religious title, position, or role) whose legitimacy or historical continuity is disputed across lineages. Example: Iyanifa (women Ifa priests) - some lineages affirm historical continuity, others claim modern innovation.

DocumentaryEvidence
Documentary Evidence

Umbrella class for archival materials (photographs, manuscripts, audio recordings, herbarium specimens) that document sacred knowledge practices. Maps to schema:CreativeWork in RDF serialization.

LineageKnowledgeGraph
Lineage Knowledge Graph
Subclass of: Entity

A named graph representing one lineage's perspective on contested knowledge. Enables multiple community-endorsed positions to coexist without requiring resolution. Core mechanism for postcustodial knowledge governance.

RelationshipAssertion
Relationship Assertion
Subclass of: Sacred Entity

A structured reification node recording a claimed relationship between two entities, including its provenance, assertion status, and any lineage scope. Enables competing claims to coexist without requiring resolution.

SacredEntity
Sacred Entity

Top-level class for any tradition-specific concept within the Iroko Framework. All module-specific classes (Plant, SpiritualEntity, RitualProcess, etc.) are subclasses of this.

SacredMedia
Sacred Media
Subclass of: Documentary Evidence

Audio, photographic, or video records of sacred knowledge, ceremonies, instruments, or practitioners. Extends DocumentaryEvidence with media-specific metadata including format, instrumentation, consent status, and depicted entities.

SacredSpecimen
Sacred Specimen

Distinguishes objects that maintain active sacred status (consecrated, in ritual use) from decommissioned objects or museum specimens. Used to track the ritual life cycle of material culture.

Concept Schemes 10 schemes
Access Level Classification 6 concepts

Hierarchical classification of access restrictions for sacred knowledge.

Community Only
access-community-only

Accessible to verified community members (practitioners, scholars with community relationships). Requires authentication.

Initiated Elder
access-initiated-elder

Requires elder designation within tradition — those recognized by their community as having authority to access lineage-specific teachings, complex ceremonial knowledge, and restricted spiritual technologies. Not merely initiated, but recognized as elder or authority holder.

Initiated Only
access-initiated-only

Accessible only to initiated practitioners whose initiation has been verified. Detailed preparation methods, quantities, combinations.

No Access
access-no-access

Operational or internal use only. Never exported to RDF. Invocation texts, secret names, internal notes.

Public - No Amplification
access-public-no-amplification

Publicly viewable but should not be amplified, shared widely, or used for commercial purposes.

Public - Unrestricted
access-public-unrestricted

Freely accessible to anyone. No authentication required.

Assertion Status 3 concepts

Controlled terms describing whether a claim is widely agreed, lineage-specific, or actively disputed. Used with iroko:RelationshipAssertion and any contested property across all modules.

Contested
assertion-contested

The claim is actively disputed; multiple contradictory positions exist across lineages.

Lineage-Specific
assertion-lineage-specific

The claim is recognized within a specific lineage or house but not universally.

Widely Attested
assertion-consensual

The claim is affirmed by multiple independent lineages or sources. Positive attestation of agreement, not merely the absence of known dispute.

Sacred Object Status 9 concepts

Classification of ritual life cycle status for sacred objects.

Awaiting Consecration
sacredStatus-awaiting-consecration

Object prepared but not yet consecrated.

Consecrated/Active Use
sacredStatus-consecrated-active

Object is consecrated and actively used in ritual practice.

Contested Consecration
sacredStatus-contested-consecration

Consecration status disputed across lineages.

Decommissioned
sacredStatus-decommissioned

Object has been ritually deactivated or decommissioned but retains historical significance.

Hereditary Custody
sacredStatus-hereditary-custody

Object has passed to a new keeper through lineage or house succession and is actively maintained. Custodial responsibility has transferred but sacred status and care protocols remain continuous.

Honored Rest
sacredStatus-honored-rest

Object has been retired from active ritual use but continues to be tended with respect and veneration. Distinguished from decommissioned in that no formal ritual deactivation has occurred — the object rests rather than being withdrawn.

Museum Specimen
sacredStatus-museum-specimen

Object held in museum or archive for documentation, not ritual use.

Replica/Teaching Object
sacredStatus-replica-teaching

Replica created for educational purposes, never consecrated.

Unknown Status
sacredStatus-unknown

Sacred status unknown or unclear from available documentation.

Haitian Vodou
tradition-haitian-vodou

The creolized sacred tradition of Haiti, encompassing multiple nanchon (nations) with distinct African, Kongo, and New World origins. Internal distinctions (Rada, Petwo, Ghede) reflect different spiritual lineages and temperaments rather than a single ethnic root.

Lucumi

New Orleans Voodoo
tradition-new-orleans-voodoo

The syncretic sacred tradition of New Orleans and Louisiana, incorporating Haitian Vodou lwa, Kongo-derived practices, Native American (Houma, Choctaw) influences, and Catholic folk magic. Contemporary practice diverges considerably from Haitian Vodou house structure. Shaped by the specific history of Louisiana's slave trade and Creole culture.

Obeah
tradition-obeah

A category of spiritual practice found across the Anglophone Caribbean (Jamaica, Trinidad, Guyana, Barbados, and others). Works with ancestral and nature spirits for healing, protection, and harm. African roots are debated — proposed origins include Igbo dibia, Akan obeayifo, and Efik sources. Systematically criminalized under British colonial law across the Caribbean; this criminalization history shapes the archival record significantly and must be considered in access and provenance documentation.

Obeah's criminalization history means archival materials documenting its practice are often court records, colonial surveillance documents, or suppressed oral accounts. Access governance and provenance tracking are especially important for Obeah-related materials.

Oyotunji
tradition-oyotunji

African-centered Yoruba revivalist tradition founded in South Carolina.

Palo Mayombe
tradition-palo-mayombe

Branch of Palo emphasizing darker or transformative spiritual work.

Palo Monte
tradition-palo-monte

Bantu-derived Kongo religious tradition in Cuba.

Pan-African Political-Spiritual
tradition-pan-african

Traditions that integrate African religious identity with political liberation frameworks.

Quimbanda

Brazilian tradition focused on exu spirits and transformative work.

Rastafari

Afrocentric religious and political movement from Jamaica.

Syncretic/Creole
tradition-syncretic

Traditions that deliberately blend multiple African, European, or Indigenous religious streams.

Trinidad Orisha
tradition-trinidad-orisha

Yoruba-derived tradition in Trinidad and Tobago.

Umbanda
tradition-umbanda

Brazilian syncretic tradition blending African, Indigenous, and Spiritist influences.

Vodou Ghede
tradition-vodou-ghede

Ghede nanchon in Haitian Vodou. Governs death, sexuality, resurrection, and the boundary between living and dead. Composite origins drawing on New World cemetery and ancestor cult practices, with Fon, Kongo, and creole influences. Neither Rada nor Petwo; a separate nation with its own liturgical character.

Vodou Petwo
tradition-vodou-petwo

Petwo nanchon in Haitian Vodou. Hot, fierce rites with Kongo, New World maroon, and creole origins. Associated with transformative and protective lwa. Distinct from but related to Rada.

Vodou Rada
tradition-vodou-rada

Rada nanchon in Haitian Vodou. Cool, sweet rites with primary Fon/Dahomey origins. Associated with elder, beneficent lwa.

Winti
tradition-winti

Afro-Surinamese tradition with Akan and other West African influences.

Yoruba (continental)
tradition-yoruba-continental

Continental Yoruba religious practices in southwestern Nigeria.

Yoruba-Derived
tradition-yoruba-derived

Traditions with primary roots in Yoruba religious culture.
